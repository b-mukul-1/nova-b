Technical Dispatch // Cloud Optimization

How we cut a client's cloud bill by 38% in 90 days.

By Marcus Vance, Chief Architect // 14 August 2026

In May 2026, an enterprise FinTech client approached NovaTech with an escalating AWS expenditure: $142,000 monthly, growing at 14% quarter-over-quarter. Despite this spend, their core settlement API suffered erratic P99 latency spikes of 340ms during morning volume peaks.

1. Profiling the True Bottlenecks

Most organizations respond to cloud cost alarms by blindly buying reserved instances. This locks in waste. Our diagnostic audit profiled compute resource utilization across their 42 microservices on Kubernetes. We discovered three structural flaws:

  • Over-provisioned pod requests: Developers configured 4 CPU cores and 16GB RAM for pods that rarely consumed more than 350m CPU and 1.2GB memory.
  • Stale on-demand node pools: The cluster ran on static m5.4xlarge instances 24/7, paying full retail rates even during low nighttime traffic.
  • Cross-AZ NAT gateway fees: Internal services were routing heavy analytics queries across availability zones, generating over $18,000 monthly in pure AWS inter-AZ transfer charges.
AUDITED EXPENDITURE REDUCTION TRAJECTORY

2. Automated Karpenter Spot Orchestration

We deployed Karpenter to manage node provisioning directly against pod scheduling requirements. By grouping stateless API pods onto diversified Spot instance pools with automated draining hooks, compute costs plunged 62% for that tier without a single dropped transaction during spot rebalances.

3. Columnar Caching on ClickHouse

Heavy audit reports that previously hammered Aurora PostgreSQL with expensive multi-table joins were migrated into a ClickHouse columnar analytics cluster. Query execution times dropped from 4.2 seconds to 180 milliseconds, allowing us to downsize the primary Aurora cluster by two instance tiers.
